Matched by barcode and SKU, not guesswork

Matched by barcode and SKU, not guesswork

Equal barcodes confirm automatically; equal SKUs confirm next. Only when neither exists does Capyo propose candidate products with a confidence score. Everything uncertain waits in the confirm queue — and a pending match can never alert or change a price.

A margin floor and an undo

A margin floor and an undo

Every rule carries a margin floor built from each variant's unit cost, and no automatic change can cross it. Approve each change yourself or let a paid plan apply it automatically. Every change is logged; undo restores the previous price while no later change exists.

The rules

A suggestion you can check, or an automation you control.

You approve or set the rules; you own your prices. Capyo shows the old price, the new price, the rival it came from and the floor in force.

  1. 1

    Matched by barcode first, SKU second

    A shared barcode or GTIN is the strongest signal, so it confirms a match on its own. An exact SKU comes next. Everything else is proposed as a candidate with a confidence score and needs your confirmation before it can do anything.

  2. 2

    The floor is part of the rule

    The floor is computed from the variant's unit cost and the minimum margin you set. A rule without a computable floor cannot auto-apply; the app lists those products as suggestion-only.

  3. 3

    Every change leaves a trail

    Each applied price stores the old price, the new price and its source. Undo restores the previous price within 30 days, while no later change to that variant exists.

How it works

Four steps from install to the first alert.

  1. 1

    Add the rival shops you watch

    Enter a domain; Capyo detects the platform and counts the products it can see there. Your plan sets how many shops you track.

  2. 2

    Sync your catalog

    Capyo reads your products and variants — price, compare-at price, SKU, barcode and unit cost — and keeps them current through Shopify webhooks and a daily sync.

  3. 3

    Review the uncertain matches

    Barcode and SKU matches confirm themselves. Everything else lands in the confirm queue with candidate products, so you confirm or reject before anything can act.

  4. 4

    Set a rule with a floor

    Choose match the lowest rival or beat it by a percentage, lower-only or both directions, a minimum margin, and approve-first or automatic.

FAQ

Before you install.

How often are rival prices checked?

Every tracked product is checked once a day. Products you mark as priority are checked every 6 hours. Six hours is the fastest cadence Capyo promises.

What happens when a match is uncertain?

It waits in the confirm queue with up to five candidate products and their confidence scores. You confirm, reject, or paste a rival product URL yourself. A pending match can never alert or change a price.

Will it change prices without me?

Only if you set an automatic rule on a paid plan, and only when the rule's margin floor is computable from the variant's unit cost. Approve-first is the default, and every change can be undone while no later change exists.

Can an automatic change go below my cost?

No. Every rule has a minimum margin floor computed from unit cost, and no automatic or approved change may cross it. Only a manual override with an explicit confirmation can go below the floor, and it is logged as a manual change.

What does it cost?

Free covers 3 products and 1 rival shop. $50/month covers 500 products and 10 rivals, $100/month 2,500 and 25, and $200/month 10,000 and 50. Variants are included; there are no per-product or overage fees.

Which rival shops work?

Public Shopify stores, WooCommerce stores and other shops whose product pages expose structured data. Capyo reads public pages only, respects robots.txt and identifies itself; a shop that blocks it is reported, never forced.

Do you read my customers or orders?

No. The scopes are read_products, write_products and read_inventory. Capyo stores no customer names, emails, addresses or order data, and requests no protected customer data.

Am I responsible for the prices it suggests?

Yes. You approve or set the rules; you own your prices. Capyo does not give legal advice, and you remain responsible for the pricing and competition rules that apply to your store.
