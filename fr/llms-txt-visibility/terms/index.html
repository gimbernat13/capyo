2. What the service does

Capyo replaces Shopify's basic /llms.txt with a product-rich template. You preview exactly what would be written — the Liquid source and a sample rendered from your own catalogue — before anything changes in your theme, and one click removes it again. The file is plain Liquid rendered by Shopify: it contains no URL of, call to, or dependency on Capyo. If Shopify has granted theme-write access, the app writes the file for you; without it, the app gives you the exact source to paste and detects a manual install by its checksum.

It runs six read-only checks on your store's public surface: is /llms.txt reachable, is the file the one the app wrote, does it list products, does /robots.txt allow the known AI crawlers, is /agents.md reachable, and is the storefront public. It never edits robots.txt or any other file it checks.

Its Web Pixel extension, running in Shopify's strict sandbox, attributes visits and orders that arrive from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini, on every plan. From your catalogue it proposes a query set in your store's primary language, which you can edit. On Growth and Pro it asks OpenAI search and Perplexity every active query on a weekly schedule, through the app's own API access, and reports which answers surfaced your store, which mentioned it and which left you out, with the change since the previous run and 12 months of history.

What it does not do: it does not generate or rewrite content; it does not edit your products, collections, campaigns or theme files other than the llms.txt template; it cannot observe AI crawler hits and never shows a crawler-visit count; and it does not promise any change in how AI assistants answer questions about your store.

7. The llms.txt template in your theme

Writing the template requires Shopify's theme-write permission. Until Shopify grants that access, the app gives you the exact Liquid source to paste into your theme and detects a manual install by its checksum; the preview works the same way either way.

You can remove the template with one click at any time, and the storefront reverts to Shopify's own default llms.txt. Uninstalling the app is not a removal. On uninstall the app makes one attempt to delete the file, but Shopify normally revokes its access before the uninstall webhook runs, so that attempt can fail. If the file is left behind it keeps working, because it is plain Liquid with no connection to Capyo — remove it before uninstalling if you do not want it to stay.
